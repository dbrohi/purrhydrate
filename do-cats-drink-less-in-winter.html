Do Cats Drink Less in Winter? Seasonal Changes in Cat Hydration

Published: October 19, 2026

Many owners notice that their cat drinks a little less when the weather turns cool, and more when it gets hot. Is that normal, and does it matter? This guide explains what usually changes with the seasons, what is nothing to worry about, and how to help your cat all year.

The short answer

Some cats do drink a little less in cool weather, and a little more in the heat. Small seasonal changes are normal and usually need no action. What matters is whether your cat's drinking, eating and litter box habits stay broadly steady, and whether a change is large or lasts for a long time.

Why drinking can change in winter

  • Less heat: cats lose less water to the warmth of the day, so the body may feel less need to drink.
  • Less activity: cats often move less in cold weather, which lowers their needs a little.
  • Dry indoor air: on the other hand, heating can dry the air indoors, and some cats still need to drink regularly.
  • Cold water: a bowl placed near a cold window or door can be less appealing, and outdoors it can freeze.
  • Diet changes: some owners feed more dry food in winter, which means the cat must drink more to get the same total water.

Why cats drink more in hot weather

In hot weather, cats lose more water by panting and evaporation, and they are more active early in the day and late in the evening. A modest rise in drinking is normal. Provide extra water bowls, keep them in the shade, and consider adding wet food or a few ice cubes in the bowl.

Heat can be dangerous. If a cat is panting heavily, drooling, very restless or weak in hot weather, move it to a cool place and call a vet at once. Heatstroke is an emergency.

How to keep your cat hydrated in cold weather

  1. Move the bowl away from drafts and cold windows, and keep it somewhere warm and quiet.
  2. Offer slightly warm water, or add a spoonful of warm water to meals.
  3. Feed some wet food, which adds water to the daily total.
  4. Check outdoor bowls often. If your cat goes outside, or you feed cats outdoors, water can freeze. A heated bowl or regular refilling helps.
  5. Keep the air from getting too dry, for example with a humidifier, if your home is very dry.

Indoor and outdoor cats

Cats that live indoors have steady conditions, so any seasonal change in drinking is usually small. Cats that spend time outside have more to cope with. In winter, outdoor water can freeze and become unavailable, and in summer it can warm up and collect dirt. If your cat goes outside, or you look after outdoor cats, check their water at least twice a day in extreme weather, use a shallow, sheltered bowl, and consider a heated bowl in freezing conditions. Bringing a bowl indoors at night is another simple option.

Water from food

Food is a quiet but important part of the picture. Wet food is mostly water, so a cat that eats it gets much of its daily fluid from meals in every season. Dry food contains very little, which means the cat has to drink the difference. If you notice your cat drinking less in cold weather, check what it is eating. A small change, such as replacing one dry meal with wet food, can keep the daily total steady without any effort from your cat.

Hot climates

In very warm places, the guidance above for hot weather applies for much of the year. Keep several bowls in cool, shaded spots, change the water more than once a day because it warms up quickly, and make sure your cat has a cool place to rest. Watch for signs that your cat is struggling with the heat, and keep a closer eye on kittens, seniors and cats with health problems.

What is normal, and what is not

Usually normalWorth a call to your vet
A small drop or rise in drinking with the weatherA big change that lasts more than a few days
Bright, active, eating as usualLethargy, hiding, or loss of appetite
Normal litter box clumpsMuch larger, much smaller or no clumps
Interest in a fresh bowl or fountainDry or sticky gums, sunken eyes, vomiting

Does the total amount change?

A healthy cat's overall water need stays around the same range through the year, roughly 50–60 mL per kilogram per day. What changes is how much comes from the bowl and how much from food. You can see your cat's range in the calculator.

Quick questions

My cat is drinking less in winter. Should I worry? Not if the change is small and your cat is well. If it also eats less, seems tired, or the change lasts, check with your vet. Our dehydration chart lists the signs.

Should I give my cat more wet food in winter? It can help, and it adds water without extra effort. See wet vs dry food for hydration.

Why does my cat ignore the bowl? It could be the bowl, the location, or the temperature of the water. See why a cat may not drink.

This article is general information, not veterinary advice or a diagnosis. If you are worried about your cat, contact your vet.
