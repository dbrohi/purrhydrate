Dehydration in Kittens: Signs and What to Do

Published: October 31, 2026

Kittens are small, so a problem with fluids can become serious faster than in an adult cat. This guide explains how kittens normally get their water, which signs to watch for, and why a vet should be involved early. It cannot replace an exam.

How kittens normally get water

Very young kittens do not drink water. They get all the fluid they need from their mother's milk, or from kitten milk replacement formula if they are hand-reared. According to Cats Protection, kittens begin to explore food and water at around four weeks and are fully weaned at around nine weeks. After weaning, a kitten drinks water and eats kitten food. Wet kitten food also gives extra fluid.

Signs of dehydration in a kitten

  • Unusually sleepy, weak or floppy
  • Not eating or not nursing
  • Dry or sticky gums
  • Sunken eyes
  • Very little urine, or much less than usual
  • Vomiting or diarrhea

These are the same signs described for adult cats by the Cornell Feline Health Center. Do not rely on the skin test alone, because it is not a dependable check at home.

Call a vet the same day if a kitten has vomiting or diarrhea, is not eating, or seems weak. Do not wait to see if it improves overnight. Go to an emergency vet if the kitten is limp, cold, has pale gums, or cannot be woken easily.

What you can do while you arrange care

  • Keep the kitten warm and calm.
  • Offer fresh water in a shallow bowl, and wet kitten food if the kitten is already eating solid food.
  • Do not give cow's milk. Cats Protection warns that it can make kittens ill.
  • Do not force water or fluids into the kitten's mouth, and do not give medicine, unless your vet has shown you how.

Helping a weaned kitten drink

Put out a few wide, shallow bowls away from food and the litter tray, and keep the water fresh. Our guide to how much water a kitten should drink explains typical amounts by age. For more ideas, see how to hydrate a cat at home.

This article is general information, not veterinary advice or a diagnosis. If you are worried about a kitten, contact your vet.
