My Cat Doesn't Drink Water but Eats Wet Food: Is That OK?

Published: November 4, 2026

Many owners notice that their cat hardly touches the water bowl but eats wet food every day. In most cases this is fine. This guide explains why, what a healthy pattern looks like, and which signs mean it is time to call your vet.

The short answer

A cat that eats wet food gets a large part of its daily water from the food. Cornell Feline Health Center notes that wet food can contain up to 80% water, so cats that eat wet food may drink less, while cats on dry food usually take more of their water by drinking. A cat that is bright, eating well and using the litter box normally is often getting enough.

How much water comes from the food

Cornell gives a general guide of about 4 ounces of water per 5 pounds of lean body weight per day. For a 10-pound cat, that is about one cup (8 ounces) in total. A small can of wet food (about 3 ounces) can hold roughly 2 ounces of water, and labels differ. So two cans a day could supply around half of a 10-pound cat's daily water before it ever visits the bowl. Your own numbers will vary with the food. Signs that things are probably fine

  • Eating its wet food well and staying active
  • Normal litter box use, with urine clumps of the usual size
  • Moist, pink gums
  • No vomiting or diarrhea

Signs to check more closely

  • Eating less as well as drinking little, or hiding and looking tired
  • Fewer or smaller urine clumps, or very dark urine
  • Dry or sticky gums
  • A mixed diet: a cat that eats only a small amount of wet food and mostly dry food needs to drink more

Cornell also lists dental problems as a reason a cat may hesitate to drink. If your cat approaches the bowl and then backs away, or drops food, mention it to your vet. To go through the signs step by step, use the cat dehydration checklist, and read when to call the vet about your cat's water intake for how quickly to act.

Call your vet if your cat is eating less, seems weak, has dry gums, or has vomiting or diarrhea. Go to an emergency vet if your cat is collapsed, has pale or blue gums, or is straining to urinate with little or no urine.

Gentle ways to encourage a little more drinking

If your cat is well and you would simply like it to drink more, Cornell lists a few ideas:

  • Keep fresh water available in places your cat can reach easily.
  • Make sure other pets are not keeping your cat away from the bowl.
  • Try a water fountain. Some cats drink more from one, but individual cats differ.
  • Add a little water to the wet food.
  • In some cases, a small amount of water from a can of tuna, or low-sodium chicken broth, can prompt a cat to drink more.

Do not force water into your cat's mouth. For more ideas, see how to hydrate a cat at home, and for the bigger picture, wet food vs dry food for cat hydration.

This article is general information, not veterinary advice or a diagnosis. If you are worried about your cat, contact your vet.
