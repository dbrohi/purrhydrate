Wet Food vs Dry Food: Which Is Better for Your Cat's Hydration?

Published: October 9, 2026

If you want your cat to get more water, the food bowl is often a better place to start than the water bowl. This guide compares wet and dry food for hydration, explains the numbers, and offers simple ways to mix the two.

The moisture numbers

Type of foodApproximate water content
Wet (canned or pouch)75–80%
Dry (kibble)about 6–10%
Semi-moist or toppersvaries, check the label

The label on the can or bag shows "moisture" in its guaranteed analysis, so you can check the exact figure for your cat's food.

What this means for water intake

Cats evolved to get much of their water from prey, which is roughly 70% water. A cat on wet food eats food that already looks like that. A cat on dry food has to make up the difference by drinking. Studies suggest that cats fed dry food tend to drink more from the bowl than cats fed wet food, but often still take in less total water. In practice, a cat on dry food has little room for error: if it drinks less than it needs, nothing in its diet makes up for it.

For example, a 10 lb (4.5 kg) cat needs roughly 227–272 mL of water a day. On two 3 oz cans of wet food, about 133 mL comes from the food, so only about 95–140 mL needs to be drunk. On 50 g of dry food, almost all of it has to be drunk. You can see the split for your own cat in the calculator.

Wet food: pros and cons

  • Pros: high moisture, often more appealing, and helpful for cats that drink little.
  • Cons: usually costs more, spoils once opened, and needs fridge storage. Follow the label for how long it can sit out and how long opened cans keep.

Dry food: pros and cons

  • Pros: convenient, keeps well, usually cheaper per serving.
  • Cons: low moisture, and free feeding can lead to overeating. The idea that dry food cleans teeth is not reliable, so it should not replace dental care.

Is wet food necessary?

No. Many healthy cats do well on dry food, especially if they drink well. But higher moisture is often recommended by vets for cats prone to urinary problems, and for some cats with kidney disease. If your cat has a health condition, ask your vet which diet is right. Do not change a prescription diet on your own.

A middle way: mixed feeding

  • Replace one dry meal a day with wet food.
  • Use wet food as a "topper" on dry food.
  • Stir warm water into dry food right before serving.
  • Add a little water to wet food to make it soupier.

How to change food safely

Cats can be fussy and have sensitive stomachs. Mix a small amount of the new food into the old, and increase it slowly over one to two weeks. Wet food usually has more calories per serving than owners expect, so adjust portions to avoid weight gain. Ask your vet about amounts, especially for senior cats or cats with health problems.

Reading the label

Pet food labels list a "guaranteed analysis," which shows a minimum or maximum moisture percentage. Wet foods typically show around 75–80%, and dry foods around 10% or less. When you compare foods, remember that the same nutrient can look very different on a wet food and a dry food label simply because of the water, so judge a diet as a whole, not by one number. Your vet can help you compare foods for your cat's age and health.

Practical tips for adding wet food

  • Start small: a teaspoon or two on top of the usual meal is enough to begin.
  • Serve at room temperature: food straight from the fridge can put cats off, so let it warm a little or add a spoonful of warm water.
  • Do not leave it out too long: wet food spoils faster than kibble, so follow the label and throw away leftovers.
  • Cover and chill opened cans, and use them within the time the label gives.
  • Buy small portions: pouches or small cans reduce waste for a single cat.

Common questions

Can I just add water to dry food? Yes, a little warm water poured over kibble just before serving adds moisture, but do not leave it sitting, since it can spoil.

Will wet food make my cat gain weight? Not by itself. What matters is the total calories your cat eats in a day. Measure portions and adjust the dry food when you add wet food.

Does wet food ruin teeth? No. Dry food is not a reliable way to clean teeth, so brushing and vet dental checks are the better protection, whatever the diet.

What about raw or fresh diets? Some owners choose them, but raw feeding carries food safety risks and diet balance matters. Talk with your vet before making that kind of change.

My cat refuses wet food. What now? Try a different texture or flavor, warm it slightly, and mix it in a little at a time. Some cats take weeks to accept a change. If your cat will not eat for more than a day, call your vet.

Does the water bowl still matter?

Yes. Even cats on wet food should always have fresh water available. See our guide to hydrating a cat at home for bowl, fountain and placement tips, and how much water a cat should drink for the amounts.

This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If you are worried about your cat, contact your vet.
